# Polycab and Atomberg: what the four decks miss

Independent review of the Polycab Independent Commercial POV, Polycab Competitor View, Atomberg Independent Commercial POV and Atomberg Competitor View (September 2026). The Havells POV was read for cross-reference only.

**Tags.** F = reported fact (company, regulator, rating agency, trade data or press). D = my arithmetic on reported numbers. I = my inference, labelled as such. Where I could not verify something, I say so.

**Limit, stated upfront.** This environment's network policy blocked direct downloads of the Polycab annual report and call transcripts, NSE/SEBI filings and the Atomberg DRHP. I worked from search-indexed extracts of those documents, an audited-financials forensic dataset (InvestSights, FY26), and every number in the four decks. That is enough to form the hypotheses below. It is not enough to close them; Section 9 lists the exact notes that would.

---

## 1. Bottom line

**Polycab.** Both decks tell a counter story. The bigger story is a balance-sheet one. FY26 was financed at both ends: ₹4,266 cr of bank acceptances funded copper on the supplier side, and dealer receivables grew nine points faster than revenue on the customer side. Only a quarter of a record ₹2,672 cr profit became operating cash. At the same time, the copper Polycab buys is being narrowed by a quality control order and is increasingly supplied by the two groups now entering its market (Aditya Birla through Hindalco and UltraTech; Adani through Kutch Copper and Praneetha). The FMEG turnaround is real in revenue but its margin sits on a solar line with a scheme end date, and the export ambition runs into a 25% US Section 232 tariff that survived the February IEEPA ruling. Rebuild both decks around one idea: **Polycab's next moat is copper and cash; the counter is where it spends them.**

**Atomberg.** Both decks adopt the DRHP's framing of "a profitable fan engine funding adjacencies". The numbers do not support it. Fans and locks grew 23% while Crompton's BLDC portfolio grew over 30%, so Atomberg very likely lost BLDC share in FY26, masked by a six-fold jump in kitchen revenue sold at a 35% segment loss. The fan segment's 11.7% margin exists only after in-house components are supplied at a loss and before ₹90-165 cr of cost that no segment carries; fully loaded, the fan business is roughly breakeven. The IPO funds two years of brand and R&D spend plus investor exits. Regulation, incumbents and a component crunch are commoditising the motor at the same time. Rebuild both decks around the question the IPO investor will ask: **does the fan business earn money fully loaded, and if not, what does the platform become?**

## 2. Polycab: seven hypotheses the decks do not state

### H1. FY26 was financed at both ends, and FY27 unwinds both

Facts:
- FY26 operating cash flow ₹679 cr against PAT ₹2,672 cr (0.25x). Free cash flow −₹801 cr after ₹1,480 cr capex. [F, audited FY26 via InvestSights]
- Receivables +37.8% against revenue +28.9%; inventory +52%; short-term borrowings ₹1,373 cr to ₹4,359 cr. [F]
- Management: Q4 working capital at 25 days against a 45-50 day steady state "due to LC-driven payable extension"; ₹4,266 cr of acceptances to unwind in FY27. [F, Q4 FY26 call extracts]
- During FY26 dealers stocked wires ahead of expected copper increases; volume slowed to low single digits in Q4 and low-to-mid single digits in Q1 FY27 as the channel destocked, against 18% for FY26. [F]
- Beneish M-score −1.61 (above the −2.22 screen line); Piotroski 3/9. [F, mechanical screens, not findings]

Arithmetic:
- Normalising working capital from 25 days back to 45-50 days on FY26 revenue absorbs ₹1,600-2,000 cr. [D]
- If management's ₹4,190 cr net cash treats acceptances as trade payables (the 25-day figure implies it does), acceptance-adjusted net cash is roughly zero (₹4,190 cr less ₹4,266 cr). [D]
- One press summary quotes cash conversion near 1.0x; the dataset says 0.25x. The gap is of the same order as the acceptances. Two honest readings of the same year differ by the classification of one line. [D]

Peer read-across, FY26 [F, InvestSights]:

| | OCF / PAT | Receivables growth vs revenue growth | DSO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Havells | 0.93x | −38% vs +4% | 13 days |
| KEI | 0.97x* | +3% vs +22% | 57 days |
| RR Kabel | 0.46x | +21% vs +29% | 37 days |
| Finolex Cables | 0.20x | +53% vs +19% | 21 days |
| **Polycab** | **0.25x** | **+38% vs +29%** | **48 days** |

*A press report puts KEI's FY26 OCF at ₹32 cr, which conflicts with 0.97x. Verify before using KEI in any exhibit.

Inference [I]: Copper's 40% rise was financed by banks on the supplier side and by Polycab on the dealer side. Part of FY26's 18% volume, and part of the organised-share move from 26-27% to 30-31%, sits in dealer godowns bought on credit ahead of price hikes; the weak Q4 and Q1 volumes are the other half of the same trade. Havells and Polycab moved dealer credit in opposite directions in the same copper spike: Havells' receivables fell by over a third (most likely a shift to bank-funded channel finance; verify), Polycab's rose by over a third. The counter war of 2026 is being fought partly with credit terms, and Polycab is the one carrying its dealers' copper.

None of this implies anything improper; it is what a copper spike does to a distribution business. It changes three things in the decks: (a) FY26 "share" is partly sell-in; (b) Project Spring's ₹6,000-8,000 cr capex is self-funded only if copper stops rising; (c) the sharpest question for Polycab's CFO is not attach rate, it is how much dealer credit bought how much share.

### H2. Polycab's copper supply is narrowing, and its new owners are Polycab's new competitors

Facts:
- Polycab accounted for 21% of India's copper cathode import shipments (Nov 2023 to Oct 2024); Hindalco 24%. [F, trade data]
- In 2021 Polycab sold its Asoj continuous copper-rod facility to Hindalco. [F]
- Copper cathode QCO (December 2024): only BIS-licensed cathode may be sold. At the industry's July 2025 complaint there were ten foreign licensees (seven Japan, two Malaysia, one Austria), four of which do not supply cathode at all, and the five domestic licensees consume their own output. [F] Amendments followed; the latest I found is dated 30 June 2026 and I have not read its text.
- Hindalco meets more than half of India's refined copper demand; Adani's Kutch Copper started at 0.5 Mtpa and targets 1 Mtpa by FY29; Vedanta's Tuticorin smelter remains shut. [F]
- Ultravolt belongs to UltraTech, an Aditya Birla company like Hindalco. Praneetha sources 100% of its copper from Kutch Copper. [F]

Inference [I]: The Competitor View treats Ultravolt and Praneetha as distribution entrants. Their deeper weapon is upstream. In a market where imports need a BIS licence held by a handful of foreign smelters, both domestic smelters belong to the two groups entering house wires. This does not mean they will starve Polycab; Hindalco has every reason to keep selling to India's largest buyer. It means the entrants' cost and certainty of copper is structurally better than Polycab's in exactly the moments that decide share: a spike, a shortage, a widening domestic premium over LME. Polycab gave up upstream rod capacity in 2021, just before its future rivals integrated forward. This is the most important competitive fact missing from both Polycab decks.

To check: Polycab's FY26 cathode sources by country and licence status; the 2026 domestic premium over LME; whether Polycab's rod or cathode purchases from Hindalco are contracted; the June 2026 QCO amendment.

### H3. Growth is copper, share is partly stocking, and high copper helps the unbranded

Facts: Q1 FY27 wires and cables revenue +39% on roughly 4% volume with copper +40%. Crisil sees FY27 industry revenue +28-30% on ~10% volume and operating profit +12-13%. [F, your Comp View]

Inference [I]: High copper widens the rupee gap between a branded coil and an unbranded or non-ISI one. Your own RUM data places unbranded wire in 46% of wire counters. The organised share Polycab reports was earned in a rising-copper year with dealer credit; FY27 is the first test of whether it holds on sell-out. Track Polycab volume against RR Kabel's (+17% in Q1) and the organised-share disclosure each quarter. The Comp View's slide 5 already makes the copper point; add the stocking point and the unbranded-gap mechanism.

### H4. The FMEG turnaround is real in revenue and dated in margin

Facts: FMEG Q1 FY27 ₹761 cr, +71%, EBIT about 8%; FY26 FMEG EBIT ₹55 cr (about 2.7%); solar is the largest FMEG line, under half of it, and more than doubled. [F] PM Surya Ghar reached 50 lakh homes and 14.8 GW by August 2026; the scheme ends March 2027; a 3-4 month extension and a successor with battery storage are under consultation. [F] Polycab has delivered 3.5 lakh+ inverters, about 1.6 GW. [F]

Arithmetic [D]: FMEG added about ₹316 cr year on year in Q1. If solar is ₹200-380 cr and grew 2.0-2.5x, it supplied between one-third and three-quarters of that increment, about half in a central case. Polycab's inverters equal at most about 7% of scheme homes (not all go to the scheme).

Devil's advocate: ex-solar FMEG still grew roughly 30-60% in the quarter. So the revenue turnaround is not only subsidy. The margin is the fragile part: a move from 2.7% to 8% in one quarter is operating leverage on volume, and the biggest volume line has an end date.

Inference [I]: The POV calls the turnaround "real" and never mentions solar. That is the first thing Polycab management would notice. Show FMEG growth and margin with and without solar, and make "FMEG margin ex-solar" the governing KPI.

### H5. The export lane has a tariff wall in its largest market

Facts: Under Section 232, from 6 April 2026 the US charges 25% on the full value of copper-intensive derivative products; from 30 July 2026 importers must report the country of smelt and cast for HTS 8544.42 and 8544.49 (insulated conductors). [F] The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on 20 February 2026; Section 232 was untouched. [F] North America was 39% of Polycab's FY26 exports and 45-50% of Q1 FY27 exports; the Middle East was 16% and has been disrupted since March. [F, your Comp View]

Inference [I]: Most Indian goods got tariff relief in February; insulated cable into the US did not. The Comp View attributes the Q1 export fall to West Asia. The structural issue is the US. Project Spring's goal of more than 10% of revenue from exports by FY30 (about 3x FY26) now needs either a non-US mix (Europe, Australia, South America) or US-side value addition. The deck should say which. I am confident in the rule; confirm each tariff line's final scope with a US customs broker before putting a number on it.

### H6. The two decks contradict each other on fans versus switchgear, and switchgear should win

The POV (slide 7) says prioritise BLDC fans. The Competitor View (slides 2, 10, 17) says switchgear before fans and lists "chasing fans before switchgear" under what to avoid. A client reading both will notice.

My call is switchgear first:
1. Route-native: 87% of wire doors stock MCBs, and MCBs are bought on electrician and contractor specification, which is Polycab's influence route. [F, RUM]
2. Profit pool: Havells earns 22.8% on switchgear. [F]
3. The fan fight is the most expensive fight on the counter in 2026: Havells doubled A&P to ₹286.5 cr in Q1 FY27 and accepted a 17% PAT decline to buy share; Crompton's BLDC grew about 44%; Atomberg spends 10.5% of revenue on brand; BEE's 2026 star reset makes five-star table stakes. [F]
4. Polycab's fan reach is 8.5% of doors with an unproven DQI (1.08, interval includes 1.0). [F, RUM]

Caveat: RUM did not measure Polycab in switchgear, so "least served" is unmeasured. Say so.

Housing adds a twist. Q2 2026 housing sales fell 6% while launches rose 7%; affordable homes were only 6% of launches and luxury 22%. [F] Wiring lags launches by two to three years, so FY27-28 wiring demand is supported, but the mix is premium [I]. That favours Legrand and Schneider-grade switches and MCBs over Polycab's entry-priced range (Etira ₹50 against Legrand ₹160). The switchgear push needs a premium tier, not only attach.

### H7. Governance context (audience-dependent)

Facts: In December 2023 the CBDT searched the Polycab group and alleged about ₹1,000 cr of unaccounted cash sales and more than ₹400 cr of cash paid by a distributor on Polycab's behalf for raw materials; Polycab said it cooperated and had received no communication on the outcome. [F] In September 2025 promoters and the Director of Commodities sold 1.55% for about ₹1,740 cr. [F] In October 2025 the CFO resigned for personal reasons and a successor was appointed. [F] FY26 audit unqualified, six KAMs, disputed statutory dues ₹170 cr. [F]

Inference [I]: None of this proves wrongdoing. It matters only because raw-material procurement sits at the centre of H1 and H2. In a deck addressed to Polycab management, leave it out. In an investor-facing version, one line in the risk register.

## 3. Polycab Independent POV: changes

**Thesis.** "Turn wires dominance into selective consumer scale" is right but incomplete. Proposed: *Polycab won share with copper-inflated revenue and dealer credit; the next five years reward whoever controls copper, cash and the counter, in that order.* Keep RUM as the proprietary lens and point it at H1 to H6.

Slide by slide:
- **S1 cover.** All three KPIs are good news. Swap one for a tension metric: "13.7%: wire doors where Polycab sells without Havells" (management audience) or "0.25x: FY26 cash conversion" (investor audience). The first is the sharper RUM hook.
- **S2 answer.** The four pillars miss cash and copper, solar and exports. Replace "Preserve the wires moat" (unfalsifiable) with "Fund the moat: copper sourcing and dealer credit". Make "FMEG ex-solar margin" the profitability gate.
- **S3.** "+68%" here against "+71%" in the Comp View for the same quarter; "2.6%" (S13) against "2.7%". Harmonise and state the basis. Add the solar decomposition.
- **S4.** DQI 1.30 is presented as Polycab's edge; the Comp View shows Havells also at 1.30. Without that, the POV overclaims. Add that RUM excludes the West, Polycab's strongest zone.
- **S6.** Strong. Add Havells' 22.8% switchgear margin so co-stocking becomes a profit argument, not only a route argument.
- **S7.** Flip "Prioritise BLDC fans" to MCBs and premium switches; move fans to "compete selectively".
- **S8.** Atomberg is the wrong comparator. Use Havells (A&P 4.4% of revenue in Q1 FY27 against Polycab's ~0.7%). The strategic question is whether Polycab can win the counter without matching Havells' A&P, not whether it should copy a D2C challenger.
- **S9.** The retailer screen is sourced to the Ultravolt POV. If Polycab sees a competitor's name in a source line, the meeting changes tone. Re-source it as "RUM retailer screen".
- **S12.** The three route archetypes are generic; fold into S6 and S7.
- **S13-14 cockpit.** Add volume share (not value), receivable days by channel, and FMEG margin ex-solar.
- **New slides:** (1) the copper chain: smelter to rod to wire, with group ownership and QCO licence status (H2); (2) cash conversion and dealer credit against peers (H1); (3) exports under Section 232, if the POV keeps an export pillar (H5).

## 5. Atomberg: eight hypotheses the decks do not state

### A1. The core is losing BLDC share, and kitchen is masking it

Facts: FY26 revenue ₹1,293.8 cr (+34.8%). Home appliances (fans and smart locks) ₹1,153 cr, +23.1% from ₹937 cr. Kitchen ₹124 cr from ₹19.3 cr (6.4x). Components external revenue ₹17.2 cr from ₹3.6 cr. [F, DRHP via press] Crompton's BLDC portfolio grew more than 30% in FY26 and about 44-45% in Q1 FY27; Orient's BLDC grew 36% in Q1 FY27. [F]

Arithmetic [D]: Of the ₹334 cr revenue increase, 65% came from home appliances, 31% from kitchen and 4% from components. If the BLDC pool grew 30-35% in FY26 (Crompton's rate) and Atomberg's fans grew about 23% or less (locks sit inside the segment), Atomberg's BLDC share fell two to four points from its 40-45%.

Inference [I]: The POV's cover metric (+34.8%) reads as the core's growth. It is not. The most important sentence missing from both Atomberg decks: *the company that created Indian BLDC is now growing slower than the incumbents it disrupted.* Caveat: Crompton grows from a smaller BLDC base; the DRHP's Redseer market sizes for FY25 and FY26 will settle the share arithmetic.

### A2. The "profitable fan engine" is a segment-accounting construct

Facts: FY26 segment results: home appliances +₹135 cr on ₹1,158 cr (11.7%); kitchen −₹43 cr on ₹124 cr; components −₹37 cr on ₹156 cr, of which only ₹17 cr is external. [F, DRHP as cited in your Comp View] Adjusted EBITDA −₹37.1 cr; net loss ₹148.9 cr; R&D ₹86.8 cr. [F]

Arithmetic [D]:
1. About 89% of components revenue (roughly ₹139 cr) is supplied in-house to the fan business, at a loss. Loading that loss back puts home appliances at about ₹98-102 cr, or 8.5-9%: Havells ECD territory, not Crompton's.
2. Segments sum to +₹55 cr, but company adjusted EBITDA is −₹37 cr. So about ₹92 cr of cost sits in no segment if segment results are before depreciation, and about ₹163 cr if they are after it (D&A about ₹71 cr in my earlier read; verify). That is 7-13% of revenue, roughly the size of the R&D line.
3. Allocating that cost by revenue, the fully loaded fan business earns roughly −4% to +2%: breakeven.

Inference [I]: The Comp View's "the fan engine pays for itself at 11.7%" and the POV's "profitable core fan engine funding multiple bets" both repeat the DRHP's segment frame. The fans do not fund the bets; the capital markets do (A3). For Atomberg's CFO or an IPO investor, this is the reframe: the question is not which adjacency to fund, but what makes the fan business profitable fully loaded. Candidate answers are scale that absorbs R&D, honest transfer pricing on components, and moving up the price ladder (your own finding that 19 of 24 BLDC fans sell below ₹5,000).

### A3. The IPO pays for operating spend and for exits

Facts: Fresh issue ₹450 cr: ₹150 cr marketing, ₹100 cr R&D, ₹90 cr debt repayment. Offer for sale of 7.65 cr shares (A91 3.77 cr, Temasek-backed V-Sciences 1.22 cr, Jungle Ventures 0.99 cr). [F] Early investors and founders sold ₹683 cr in secondaries since 2019; A91 invested ₹143 cr and sold ₹445 cr while retaining 21%. First ESOP liquidity programme launched. [F]

Arithmetic [D]: 56% of the fresh issue is P&L spend (brand plus R&D), about ₹125 cr a year for two years; 76% is P&L spend plus debt repayment. Net loss widened by ₹31 cr while adjusted EBITDA improved by ₹14 cr, so about ₹45 cr of below-EBITDA charges rose.

Inference [I]: The ₹45 cr is most likely ESOP and pre-IPO charges; verify. Strategically, Atomberg's 10.5% brand intensity is IPO-funded through FY28. After that, the fan business must fund it or the intensity falls. The POV's slide 8 ("₹150 cr for marketing") should say so plainly.

### A4. The brand-money gap just widened

Facts: Havells' Q1 FY27 A&P was ₹286.5 cr, double the prior year and 4.4% of revenue; PAT fell 17%. [F] Atomberg's FY26 A&SP was ₹135 cr. [F]

Arithmetic [D]: Havells spent 2.1x Atomberg's full-year brand budget in one quarter. Atomberg plus IPO money is about ₹210 cr a year; Havells' Q1 run-rate is about ₹1,150 cr.

Inference [I]: Havells is buying fan share with its balance sheet. "Highest intensity in the set" stops mattering when a rival's absolute spend is five times larger. Update the Comp View's slide 5 and the POV's A&SP framing.

### A5. The motor moat is being commoditised on three fronts at once

- **Regulation.** BEE's 2026-28 table sets five-star at 6.5 CMM/W or better for 1050-1500 mm fans, which in practice only BLDC reaches. [F] Every brand's five-star fan becomes BLDC and the energy claim stops differentiating [I]; Polycab already cited a 4-5% fan price correction from the new norms. [F, your Comp View]
- **Price ladder.** The DRHP's 46% share of premium fans uses Redseer's ₹3,498 premium threshold; Atomberg's median BLDC price is ₹3,974 and 19 of its 24 BLDC fans sell below ₹5,000. [F] Havells' BLDC range starts at ₹2,719 and Orient's at ₹2,749. [F] "Premium leader" really means "the cheapest premium brand" [I]. As incumbents take BLDC below ₹3,498, Atomberg is squeezed from below while Crompton, Orient and Bajaj keep 56-89% of their BLDC SKUs above ₹5,000.
- **Components.** Imported or globally sourced components rose from 8.8% to 16.7% of revenue. [F] 2026 lead times for MOSFETs and MCUs run 20-52 weeks on AI and automotive demand. [F, distributor reports] Crompton lost about ₹200 cr of primary sales to supply tightness in Q1 FY27. [F] In an allocation market the biggest buyer is served first, and in-house PCBA capacity does not make chips [I]. India's REPM magnet scheme took bids in August 2026 with a two-year gestation [F], so no domestic magnet relief before about FY29.

Precision flag: the Comp View says rare-earth magnets "sit in every BLDC motor". Many BLDC ceiling-fan motors use ferrite magnets. I do not have reliable data on Atomberg's magnet chemistry; source the claim or soften it to "magnets and semiconductors".

### A6. Components and the Voltas JV are a utilisation fix, and control sits with Voltas

Facts: Capacity of 13.88 mn motor assemblies, 15.84 mn PCBAs and 6.0 mn stator windings a year; Chakan builds 6.6 mn consumer-appliance units; components external revenue ₹17 cr. [F] Voltas JV: 50:50 equity, Voltas nominates three of five directors, 2.8 mn room-AC compressors a year in Chennai, Voltas as anchor customer. [F]

Inference [I]: I have not seen fan unit volumes. At ₹1,153 cr and a realisation near ₹3,000 per fan, volume would be roughly 3.5-4 mn units, which would put the motor platform at perhaps a quarter to a third utilised for own use. That explains why components exists and why the JV matters. The DRHP's capacity-utilisation table will confirm or kill this in five minutes. The strategic read: Atomberg's most consequential 2026 announcement is not a fan; it is that its motor and drive know-how is being monetised in a JV it does not control. The POV calls components "B2B platform economics", which undersells the tension.

### A7. The purifier bet rests on contested IP and an unpriced warranty

Facts: Eureka Forbes sued alleging Intellon uses its patented TDS-adjustment and customisable-taste features; the Supreme Court moved the suit to the Bombay High Court to be heard with Atomberg's groundless-threats suit. [F] Eureka launched four-year free-service 4X purifiers at ₹37,000-38,000. [F]

Arithmetic [D]: 0.59% of a ₹117 bn market (retail value) is about ₹69 cr at retail, perhaps ₹45-50 cr of revenue.

Inference [I]: The POV's purifier-annuity slide ignores the litigation, which is the single biggest risk to that slide. And "no AMC" moves service cost into a warranty provision. Check the DRHP's warranty-provision movement against purifier units to see whether the promise is priced.

### A8. Offline, Atomberg is repeating the pattern your own wires data warns against

Facts: Touchpoints rose from 27,116 to 46,932 (+73%) in two years while offline revenue per touchpoint fell from ₹2.07 lakh to ₹1.75 lakh (−16%). [F/D, your Comp View] In your Polycab Comp View, RR Kabel and KEI "bought reach in thinner doors" and show negative value alpha (DQI 0.87 and 0.79). [RUM]

Inference [I]: Your RUM wires lesson predicts Atomberg's offline productivity decline. And 46.4% of Atomberg's 2023 fan doors also stock Polycab: Atomberg's offline route runs through wire-led electrical counters where Polycab, Havells and Crompton (now also selling Armor wires) hold the relationship. That is where the POV's "next 10,000 touchpoints" should be tested.

## 8. The consolidated hypothesis: the counter is converging, and it is being won with money

Wire brands sell fans (Polycab, Havells). Fan brands now sell wires (Crompton's Armor, Bajaj's Secura). 76% of wire doors stock fans, and nearly half of Atomberg's fan doors stock Polycab. When categories converge on one counter, the winner is not the best product in any one category. It is the one that can fund the counter: credit to dealers, brand money, and secure inputs. 2026 made all three expensive at once: copper up 40%, an A&P war led by Havells, and chip allocation driven by AI demand.

- For **Polycab**, the scarce resource is cash tied up in copper. Its advantage is scale and dealer relationships. Its threat is copper-integrated entrants with patient group balance sheets.
- For **Atomberg**, the scarce resource is fully loaded profit. Its advantage is engineering speed. Its threat is incumbents with cash turning BLDC into a standard product.

All four decks analyse the counter well and the money poorly. The single upgrade that makes them land is to put a money exhibit beside every counter exhibit.

Two lines worth putting on a cover:
- Polycab: *The counter is shared; the copper is not, and Polycab's rivals now own it.*
- Atomberg: *Atomberg's fans are profitable only before the costs that make them Atomberg's.*

## Key risks to these hypotheses

- **Polycab H1** softens if the annual report classifies acceptances as trade payables in the cash-flow statement (then OCF is near 1x and the dataset is misreading it). The ₹4,266 cr unwind and the dealer-credit build stand either way.
- **Atomberg A2** depends on whether segment results are struck before or after depreciation and what "unallocated" contains. Under both readings the fully loaded fan margin sits between slightly negative and breakeven, unless the DRHP already allocates R&D inside segments, in which case the unallocated gap is something else and must be named.
- **RUM** is urban UP and Tamil Nadu in 2023; it predates Atomberg's offline doubling and excludes Polycab's West.

## 9. What to pull from primary documents (one to two hours of reading)

**Polycab FY26 annual report:** cash-flow statement and the note on acceptances or supplier finance; receivables ageing and any channel-finance arrangements; inventory split between copper and finished goods; commodity-hedging note; contingent liabilities (income-tax matters); FMEG segment note; related-party transactions with distributors.
**Polycab Q1 FY27 transcript:** solar revenue, FMEG margin drivers, acceptances outstanding at June.
**Atomberg DRHP:** Ind AS 108 segment note (unallocated items, depreciation treatment); capacity-utilisation table; restated cash flow; ESOP charge; warranty-provision movement; Redseer BLDC market size for FY25 and FY26; material contracts (Voltas term sheet); litigation chapter (Eureka Forbes; the April 2026 Stove Kraft matter, which I have seen listed but not read).
